# Documentation audit: does the prose tell the truth?

You are auditing the documentation of an existing Lean 4 repository: module docstrings,
declaration docstrings, and the published documents that cite declarations. Linters may check that
docs are *present*; you judge whether they are **useful and honest**.

Work only on the files you are given. Report findings; do not edit anything unless asked.

## Repository facts

- Repo root: `/home/skoonce/lean/klawd_max_power/lean4-jax-mlir`
- Published claims live in `README.md`, `RESULTS.md`, `BENCHMARK.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`, and the
  blueprint under `blueprint/src/`. These are what readers actually believe.
- This repo has been through correctness, reuse and proof-quality passes. Proofs were reworked;
  **the prose around them often was not.** Stale docstrings are the most likely finding class.
- Files marked `Generated by …`, `AUTO-GENERATED`, `DO NOT EDIT`, or `@generated`: judge the
  docstring the *generator* emits (in `scripts/`), once, not each copy.

## What counts as a finding

In descending order of seriousness:

1. **Overclaiming.** A docstring or published document that promises more than the statement
   delivers — a finding **even when the theorem is correct**. The usual shapes: the prose drops a
   hypothesis, generalises from a fixed dimension or one architecture, says "for all inputs" where
   the theorem covers one, says "proved" of something assumed, or calls a bound tight that is not.
2. **Wrong or stale.** The docstring describes a statement, name, or proof that no longer exists.
   Includes cross-references to renamed or deleted declarations, and "no sorry / axiom-clean"
   claims — verify each such claim rather than trusting it.
3. **Describes the proof, not the result.** A declaration docstring should say what the object is
   or what the theorem establishes. Proof narrative belongs in comments inside the proof.
4. **Missing where it matters.** A substantive file with no module docstring saying what lives
   there and why; an undocumented public definition, main theorem, non-obvious abbreviation, or
   exported instance whose purpose is not clear from its name.
5. **Process narrative instead of mathematical purpose.** A module docstring should say what the
   material is *for*, what it feeds, and what a reader needs to use it. Layer or phase numbers,
   run IDs and dates, PR or pass history ("after the reuse pass…"), and status notes about work in
   flight do not belong there: they date badly and nothing updates them when the project moves.
   That history belongs in commit messages, where this repo already records it in detail.
   Mathematical references and citations stay in the file.
6. **Copied without adapting.** A docstring lifted from a sibling declaration or from Mathlib
   that still names the original's object, variables, or hypotheses.

## How to check

For every docstring in scope, read the statement it sits on and compare them line by line:
quantifiers, hypotheses, the object, the conclusion's strength (`≤` vs `=`, one direction vs
`↔`). For every claim in a published document that names or links a declaration:

```bash
grep -rn "<DeclarationName>" --include=*.lean LeanMlir/ tests/   # does it still exist, and where
grep -rn "<DeclarationName>" README.md RESULTS.md BENCHMARK.md blueprint/src/   # who cites it
```

Then read the actual statement, and ask what a reader of the document would conclude — and
whether the theorem supports that conclusion.

## Evidence standard

Quote the prose and quote the statement, side by side, and name the exact gap. Give the corrected
wording. "Could be clearer" is not a finding.

## What is NOT a finding

- Whether a docstring exists on an obvious private helper.
- Tone, grammar, or length — unless it misleads.
- Mathematical correctness of the theorem itself. Different audit; if you spot one, note it as a
  lead for the correctness pass and move on.

## Output

Ranked most misleading first:

```
### <path>:<line> — <declaration or document section>

**Kind:** overclaim | stale | proof-narrative | process-narrative | missing | copied
**Says:** "<the prose>"
**Actually states:** <the statement, or "declaration no longer exists">
**Fix:** <corrected wording>
```

End with the overclaims gathered in one list, since those are the ones to fix before anyone reads
the published results again.
