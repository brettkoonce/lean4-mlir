\section{Image generation --- demo: low-dose CT synthesis on LIDC, held out on Mayo}
\label{sec:bestiary_ct_synthesis}

Networks that make images rather than read them. A tenth of the X-ray dose
gives a chest CT a tenth of the photons, and the reconstruction turns the
shortfall into grain and streaks; the task the vendors sell is to hand the
radiologist back the routine-dose image. The demo trains the Part~1 UNet to
do that three ways, scores each the way the field does, and then asks the
two questions that score does not answer: does the output still
\emph{look like a CT scan}, and what does the same network do on a scanner
it has never seen.

\textbf{The data.} LIDC-IDRI (Armato et al.\ 2011; Appendix~\ref{app:data})
is the training set and the instrument: 300 patients' chest scans, each
slice paired with a simulated low-dose version --- Poisson counts in the
projection domain at a photon budget calibrated to the slice's own noise,
filtered back-projection, the scanned slice itself as the target --- and
four radiologists' outlines of every nodule, 395 on the validation
patients, 242 of them under 3\,mm. The dose is a tenth of routine here, which
is what the held-out set has; a quarter-dose build with two realisations a
slice is run beside it, and a second tenth-dose build of the same slices,
its back-projection window fitted to Mayo's noise spectrum peak for peak,
is the kernel rows' training set; the dose range rebuilt under that
window trains the rows that take both at once. Mayo's LDCT-and-Projection-data (Moen et al.\ 2021)
is the test: 50 chest patients' routine-dose scans and the vendor's own
projection-domain insertion at 10\,\% --- other patients, another
institution's Siemens scanners, a sharp B50f kernel where most of LIDC is
soft-kernel GE. No Mayo image trains a net or picks a knob. The network is
the BraTS UNet of \S\ref{sec:bestiary_segmentation} at one channel in and
one out (\texttt{lidc-denoise-train}, 7.85M parameters), trained three ways
on $256^2$ crops: to the scanned slice under squared error (the MSE arm);
to a \emph{second} low-dose realisation of the same slice, never a clean one
(Noise2Noise, Lehtinen et al.\ 2018); and as the generator --- the same
layers under the DDPM step of \S\ref{sec:bestiary_diffusion}, predicting the
noise added to the residual $(\mathrm{full} - \mathrm{low})/100$\,HU --- or, in
the rows that say so, the residual itself --- with the
low-dose slice concatenated as a conditioning channel, sampled by DDIM at
$\eta = 1$ in 50 steps (\texttt{lidc-denoise-sample}). The MSE net is an hour
and a quarter on one card, the generator two and a half to three; a sample
is a second a slice. Beside them the pre-learning bracket: a Gaussian, total variation and
BM3D, each knob tuned on the training patients. Table~\ref{tab:ct_synthesis}
is the result, scored on the same 1,605 validation slices (every nodule
slice and 400 others) and on 1,000 Mayo slices, twenty a patient.

\medskip\noindent \textbf{The left block is the first result.} The two learned
denoisers beat every filter by twenty HU and keep the nodules: $97.5\%$ of
the contrast over all 395 and $94\%$ on the sub-3\,mm marks, where the
filters keep $49$--$76\%$ --- what a classical filter smooths away first is
exactly the finding a reader is looking for. But the MSE net is soft. Its
output carries $56\%$ of the scanned slice's grain variance at $52\%$ of its
frequency, because squared error to a noisy target trains the posterior
\emph{mean}, and the mean of many plausible scans is smoother than any one
of them. Noise2Noise, which never sees a clean slice, lands on the same
net: $37.1$ against $37.5$\,HU at a quarter dose, the same grain to the
second decimal, so nothing in the softness came from the target --- it is
what regression to the mean is. The generator puts the grain back. One
sample carries $110\%$ of the scanned slice's grain variance at $97\%$ of
its frequency and the scanned slice's own count of small blobs (82 a slice
against 80), and it keeps the nodules as the regression net does. It pays
for this exactly as a draw from the posterior must: its RMSE is
$\sqrt{2}$ times the mean's ($73.4$ against $\sqrt{2} \times 46.1 = 65.3$
here; $54.1$ against $53.0$ at a quarter dose, where the sampler is better
calibrated), and of the blobs it draws, 42 a slice are in no scanned slice
--- six times the MSE net's seven. Four samples of the same slice disagree
by 32\,HU inside a nodule, 17 in plain lung and 23 in soft tissue, a
distribution's spread rather than a measurement's error bar. The texture is
real and so is the price: a sampler trades the regression net's twenty HU
for a grain a reader would recognise and findings the reader cannot trust
one draw of. Figure~\ref{fig:ct_synthesis} is what that looks like.

\noindent \textbf{The right block is the second.} On the scanner the nets
never saw, every regression arm crosses over: the MSE net takes 235\,HU of
inserted noise to 112, with no slice left worse than its input, and a
Gaussian tuned on LIDC does better still at 93. What it leaves is Mayo's
noise passed through as anatomy: its output carries $1.8\times$ the
scan's grain variance at $1.1\times$ the scan's frequency, because
Mayo's noise is sharper than anything it was fitted to --- the inserted
noise peaks at $0.425$\,mm$^{-1}$ against our $0.330$, the B50f kernel
against LIDC's soft GE --- and grain at a frequency the net has never seen
as grain, it keeps. Either of two training sets shows it that noise. The
one spanning every dose from 3 to 25\,\% lands at 82\,HU, the best arm on
Mayo, for two HU at home; the one whose pairs were reconstructed through a
window fitted to Mayo's spectrum, peak for peak, lands at 84; both at once
land at 83 with the range row's median of 68, so the twenty-eight HU are
bought once, by either, and the grain left in the Mayo output is
$0.4$--$0.6\times$ the scan's under any of the three. In no cell of the
table is a regression net worse than its input: what a mean misses it
leaves in place, and it adds nothing.

\medskip\noindent \textbf{The generator} fails differently. Pooled over
the thousand it is twice as far from the scan as the input, and one slice
in four comes out worse than it went in --- half of
the slices with 250--350\,HU of inserted noise and all but two of the 65
above 350: on those slices the sampler does not denoise, it runs away.
The grain a draw puts back is its training set's, in character and in
amount, and the two move separately. Move the character --- the kernel row
--- and the generator follows: its share of Mayo's slices worse than the
input falls from one in four to one in twenty and its median heavy slice
from 132 to 100\,HU; on LIDC itself the generator trained on the softer
kernel, shown the sharper, is worse than its input on 29\,\% of the heaviest
slices, while the one trained on the sharper transfers down. Move the
amount past what it saw and no training set here reaches it. The fifty
heaviest Mayo slices carry two to four times the noise of anything in a
tenth-dose LIDC build, and on those the kernel generator runs further than
before --- ten slices past 5,000\,HU are the four-digit cell; the range row
lowers the typical heavy slice two- to three-fold and leaves a quarter of
the heaviest worse than the input; and the net trained across the doses on
Mayo's kernel and told the level, the one trained for exactly those slices,
is the worst row in the table, worse than its input on 49\,\% of Mayo's
and on one slice in eight of its own validation set, at doses it trained
at. On those slices it does not drift, it diverges: on the slice of
Figure~\ref{fig:ct_synthesis} a patch of lung saturates and the rest is
clean, and pooled over the set the divergence is $10^{29}$\,HU. LIDC has
such slices of its own, three low-current GE patients on whose slices
every generator is worse than the floor one time in three and the
regression net never is.

\medskip\noindent \textbf{What runs away} is the noise estimate. The step
of \S\ref{sec:bestiary_diffusion} turns the net's $\hat\epsilon$ into an
estimate of the clean residual,
$\hat x_0 = (x_t - \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\hat\epsilon)/\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$,
and moves toward it, so an error in $\hat\epsilon$ reaches $\hat x_0$
multiplied by $\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}/\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$, the state's
noise-to-signal ratio, a hundred at the chain's start; the state itself is
carried forward with a coefficient
$\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}/\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$ above one, and
$\hat\epsilon$ is what cancels the growth. Inside the training distribution
the error is small and the next step corrects it; on a slice noisier than
anything it was fitted to it is not, the state handed on is further from
anything the net has seen, and the next error is larger. The
sampler's settings do not reach this, because the error is the net's: on
the same checkpoint a deterministic chain ($\eta = 0$) diverges on every
slice, the lightest included --- the stochastic one's fresh noise had
kept it near its training distribution --- twenty steps in place of fifty
diverge sooner, capping the level the net
is told at its training median changes nothing on the heavy slices, and
clipping $\hat x_0$ to the HU range at every step, the usual guard and the
row beneath, bounds the output and leaves 41\,\% of Mayo's slices worse
than their input, the same slices.

\medskip\noindent \textbf{The same net can predict the slice} instead.
With the loss on the residual itself rather than on the noise, everything
else the recipe above and the same fifty steps --- the step recovers
$\hat\epsilon$ from $\hat x_0$ where it needs it --- the net's error enters
the next state scaled by at most $\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}$, the state is
carried forward with a coefficient at most one, and there is nothing to
compound under. The two rows \emph{predicting the clean slice} are the
kernel generator and the both-at-once generator retrained that way.
Neither leaves a slice of Mayo's thousand worse than its input; their
median slice is 85 and 89\,HU where the best $\epsilon$ generator's was
100, their worst is 575 where the $\epsilon$ generators ran to $10^{30}$,
and on the 651 slices up to 250\,HU of noise their grain is
$0.75$--$1.1\times$ the scan's at $0.75$--$1.0\times$ its frequency, where
the $\epsilon$ generator's sat a decade above ---
Figure~\ref{fig:ct_synthesis} has the draw of the same slice beside the
saturated patch, and the spectrum on the scan's. Pooled they sit at 118
against the regression net's 83, $1.2\times$ on the light slices and
$1.5\times$ on the heaviest, where they leave half the noise in place.
At home one sample carries $93$--$95\%$ of the
scanned slice's grain variance at $91$--$94\%$ of its frequency, invents
21--23 blobs a slice against the $\epsilon$ generator's 35 and the MSE
net's six, and keeps $96.4$--$96.7\%$ of nodule contrast, a point under the
others; its RMSE is $57$\,HU against the MSE net's $45$, $1.27\times$ where
the $\epsilon$ generator paid $1.56\times$ and a posterior draw is allowed
$\sqrt{2}$. The two rows are alike in every cell: the kernel sets the
grain, and the dose range and the level channel add nothing.

\medskip\noindent A net trained to the mean generalises across dose once
it has seen the range. A net trained to draw puts back its training set's
grain in character and in
amount, and how far a draw goes wrong on a slice outside that set is set
by what the net predicts: the noise, which the step scales by the state's
noise-to-signal ratio, or the slice, which it scales down. For the
vendor's task the number is the regression net's, 83\,HU to 118; for the
synthesis it is the generator predicting the slice, with the invented
column read beside the grain.

\medskip\noindent What this is not: a clinical denoiser, or a reader study.
Nodule contrast kept is one instrument on reader-outlined findings, not a
detection rate; the low dose is simulated on the training side, on a
parallel-beam geometry under two kernels, a textbook window and one
fitted to Mayo's spectrum, where every scanner has its own;
and the number for a scanner the net has not seen is the one on the
right.

\medskip\noindent The catalogue that closes this section is the rest of
image generation: networks whose output is a novel image rather than a
corrected one. Two families besides the diffusion of
\S~\ref{sec:bestiary_diffusion}, variational autoencoders and GANs; their
backbones overlap heavily with segmentation (the UNet again), and the demo's
generator does Pix2Pix's job, image to image on a UNet, with the adversary
replaced by the noise schedule.
